###### DEPOSIT GROWTH \[const applyBehaviour]



* Bank rate is modeled, so bank rate feeds thru into deposits.
* Baseline deposit growth (simulation line 553) is 2.4% per year. that's the growth if your rate = competitor rate
* Competitor retail interest rate is bank rate minus a margin. Competitors' interest rates approach that target slowly, over 4 months, with some noise
* competitor corporate rate is the retail rate + 50bp
* corporate deposit growth is purely a function of difference from competitor rate
* deposit growth is clamped at a max of 8% hardcoded in baseconfig.ts
* Exogenous variable: bank rate.



meta.behaviour.depositSegment === 'corporate'

          ? state.market.competitorCorporateDepositRate ?? state.market.competitorRetailDepositRate

          : state.market.competitorRetailDepositRate;



###### LOAN GROWTH \[const applyBehaviour]

Competitor mortgage benchmark is the market.competitorMortgageRate

Competitor corporate loan benchmark is riskFreeLong + corporateloanspread

Loan growth is loanbaselinegrowth + an elasticity \* relative difference between benchmark and rate you set

Loan shrinkage is clamped at -0.02 per month (simulation.ts line 598) and growth is clamped at 0.05 in baseconfig.ts

Loans can grow by an amount corresponding to available cash

Loan shrinkage is modelled as a cash inflow.

Exogenous variables: riskFreeLong, mortgageSpread, corporateloanspread,market.competitorMortgageRate

 	riskFreeLong: 30y gilt \[ukMarketModel]

 	mortgage spread: is modelled, there's a complex creditspread calculation

 	corporate spread: is modelled

###### Cashflow Statement \[const buildStatements]

Operating cashflow is cash paid out on deposit liabilities, short term wholesale funding, and repo; and cash in from loans and gilts (and reserves?)

investing cashflow is changes in balances of gilts, either through purchase/sale or losses

financing cashflow is cash out on long term funding and capital (dividends and capital issuance)



cashflow is the change in the amount of these things not including losses.



operating cashflow is from the income statement.
